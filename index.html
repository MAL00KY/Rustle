Say it, and it happens.

Hold two keys, say what you mean, and let go. The words appear wherever your cursor is. Say the name of an app and it opens. It all runs on your own computer, so nothing you say is sent anywhere, and there is no account to make.

Get it from the Microsoft Store Read the privacy policy

Free. Windows 10 and 11. There is a Pro version if you use it every day.

What it does

Typing Dictate into any app in Windows: a message, an email, a document, a code editor. If there is nowhere to type, Rustle puts the words on your clipboard instead.
Commands Say the name of an app and it opens, or say "take a screenshot" or "next track". Start with "Hey Rustle" and you do not need to touch the keyboard at all. You write the phrases yourself.
Dictionary For the names it keeps getting wrong. Spell each one the way you want it written, and Rustle shows the engine your spelling before it listens.
Snippets Say a short phrase and get a long one: an address, a link, the paragraph you find yourself typing every week.
The hub Everything you have said, searchable, with how much you wrote. Delete any line you would rather not keep.

Nothing leaves your computer

There is no Rustle server, and no account.

Speech recognition runs on your own machine. Your voice is never uploaded, and neither are your words.

Rustle turns speech into text with whisper.cpp, an open-source engine that runs locally. The engine comes with Rustle, so dictation works with no internet connection.

Everything Rustle keeps, and where it keeps it, is listed on the privacy page. It takes a couple of minutes to read.

Getting it

Rustle is free on the Microsoft Store.

What happens on first run

Rustle looks at your computer and offers the best setup it can run. The listening engine is already inside the app, so it can hear you straight away. On a computer with an NVIDIA graphics card, it offers a faster engine and a more accurate model to download first, and says how big the download is. You can start with what is there instead. Then a short introduction shows you which keys to hold.

What it needs

  • Windows 10 (version 1809) or Windows 11, 64-bit.
  • A microphone.
  • About 1 GB of disk space. The faster setup for a graphics card adds about 2.5 GB, and Rustle Pro's writing model about 2.5 GB more. Both are optional.

Questions

Does it work offline?

Yes. Dictation and commands work with no internet connection. Rustle only goes online if you choose to download something extra, such as the faster engine for a graphics card.

Is it really free?

Yes. The free version does not expire and does not need an account. Rustle Pro is a separate one-off purchase that tidies up your writing and works in other languages.

How accurate is it?

Good enough to write with, and it improves when you teach it. Put the names it keeps getting wrong into the dictionary and it starts spelling them your way. Like all speech recognition it still makes mistakes, so check anything important before you send it.

Can I stop it listening?

Yes. It records while you hold the keys and stops when you let go. Out of the box it also listens for its own name, which keeps the microphone open. That sound is not saved and never leaves your computer, and you can switch it off in Settings, under Commands, with Listen for "Hey Rustle" without holding anything. The privacy policy explains exactly what happens to it.
