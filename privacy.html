Rustle

Privacy policy

Last updated 13 September 2026

Rustle does not send your voice, or anything you say, anywhere.

There is no Rustle server and no account. Nothing you dictate is uploaded or analysed, and nobody sees it but you. The speech recognition runs on your own computer.

The rest of this page sets out what Rustle touches and where it keeps things, so you can check it for yourself instead of taking it on trust.

What Rustle records

Your voice

While you hold the keys, Rustle records from your microphone into a temporary file on your computer. It passes that file to the speech engine, which also runs on your machine and cannot be reached from outside it, and then deletes the recording. Nothing is uploaded.

Listening for "Hey Rustle" works differently, and it is switched on when you install Rustle. Rustle can only hear its name if it is already listening, so while this setting is on, the microphone stays open. The last thirty seconds of sound are kept in memory and continually written over. Rustle keeps checking the most recent two or three seconds for its name. Stretches with no voice in them are skipped. The rest are written to a temporary file for the speech engine to read, and deleted as soon as it has read them. None of that sound is saved, and none of it leaves your computer.

If you would rather the microphone stayed closed until you hold the keys, turn off Settings → Commands → Listen for "Hey Rustle".

What you said

Once your speech has been turned into words, Rustle writes those words down so the hub can show your history and word counts. Each entry also records the time, how long you spoke, and the name of the app you were dictating into. Commands you say out loud are recorded the same way.

This is a plain text file on your computer. It is not encrypted, so anyone who can use your Windows account can read it, as they could any document you save. You can delete a single line from the hub, or clear all of it from Settings → Your data → Forget.

Your settings and your licence

Your commands, dictionary, snippets and preferences are kept in the same place, so Rustle remembers them. If you buy Rustle Pro, the email address and licence key you enter are stored there as well. Rustle checks the key on your own computer and never sends it anywhere to be checked.

The programs installed on your computer

When you press Add from this PC on the Commands page, Rustle reads the list of programs and games installed on your computer so you can choose which ones to open by voice. It reads the list when you ask, and it does not send it anywhere.

Where all of this lives

Everything above is stored on your computer, in one folder:

C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Roaming\Rustle

If you installed Rustle from the Microsoft Store, Windows keeps it in that app's own private folder instead. Settings → Your data → Show me my files opens the right folder either way. You can read, copy, back up or delete any of it whenever you like, and uninstalling Rustle offers to remove it.

What leaves your computer

Rustle goes online for the three reasons below. None of them sends anything you have said or typed.

Downloads you ask for

The speech engine is too big to include in the small download, so Rustle fetches it the first time you set it up, directly from GitHub and Hugging Face. The writing model for Rustle Pro comes from the same two places, and only if you switch that feature on. As with any download, those services can see your IP address and which file was requested. They receive nothing else from Rustle.

A copy from the Microsoft Store already has the speech engine inside it, so it skips that download unless you later ask for the version that runs on an NVIDIA graphics card.

Checking for a newer version

A copy downloaded from this website asks GitHub once a day whether a newer version of Rustle has been released. The request includes the version number you have, and GitHub can see your IP address, as with any web request. Nothing else is sent. You can turn this off in Settings → Rustle itself → Look for new versions. A copy from the Microsoft Store never does this, because the Store takes care of its updates.

Websites you open

A command that opens a website, or a button in Rustle that links to this site, opens the page in your browser, the same as clicking a link. Rustle does nothing else with it.

Buying Rustle Pro

Rustle Pro is paid for on a checkout page run by a payment company, not by Rustle. The email address and payment details you enter there go to that company and are covered by its own privacy policy. Rustle never sees your payment details.

Permissions Rustle asks for

  • Your microphone, so it can hear you. Windows asks the first time, and you can take the permission away at any time in Windows privacy settings. Rustle records while you hold the keys, and keeps the microphone open while "Hey Rustle" is switched on. Your voice, above, explains what happens to that sound.
  • Watching for your keys, so it knows when you are talking to it. This has to work while you are in another app, so Rustle watches the keyboard across Windows. It looks only for the keys you chose, and it does not read or store anything else you type.
  • Typing into other apps, so your words appear where your cursor is. Rustle types them in the way a keyboard would. A few programs, mostly command windows, do not accept that, and for those Rustle pastes the words and then puts your clipboard back the way it was. If there is nowhere to type at all, it leaves the words on your clipboard so you can paste them yourself. To tell whether there is anywhere to type, it asks Windows what kind of box your cursor is in. It never reads what is written there.

Children

Rustle is not made for children. It does not collect information about anyone, so it holds nothing about children either.

Changes to this policy

If Rustle changes what it does with your information, this page changes first, along with the date at the top. If a future version would send anything about you, or anything you have said, off your computer, it will ask you first and you can say no.
