Privacy policy

Last updated 26 August 2026

Rustleaf does not send your voice, or anything you say, anywhere.

There is no Rustleaf server. There is no account. Nothing you dictate is uploaded, analysed, or seen by anyone but you. The speech recognition runs on your own computer.

That is the whole policy in two sentences. The rest of this page explains exactly what Rustleaf touches and where it is kept, because a promise like that is only worth anything if you can check it.

What Rustleaf records

Your voice

While you hold the keys, Rustleaf records from your microphone to a temporary file on your own computer. That file is passed to the speech engine — also running on your computer, reachable only from your computer — and is deleted immediately afterwards. It is never uploaded.

What you said

Once your speech has been turned into words, Rustleaf writes those words down so the hub can show you your own history and word counts. Alongside each entry it records the time, how long you spoke, and the name of the app you were dictating into. Commands you say out loud are recorded the same way.

This is a plain text file on your computer. It is not encrypted, and anyone who can use your Windows account can read it — the same as any document you save. You can delete any single line from the hub, or clear the lot from Settings → Your data → Forget.

Your settings

Your commands, dictionary, snippets and preferences are kept in the same place, so Rustleaf can remember them between sessions.

Where all of this lives

Everything above is stored on your own computer, in one folder:

C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Roaming\Rustleaf

If you installed Rustleaf from the Microsoft Store, Windows keeps it in that app’s own private folder instead. Either way, Settings → Your data → Show me my files opens the exact location. You can read, copy, back up or delete any of it at any time. Uninstalling Rustleaf offers to remove it.

What leaves your computer

Two things, both of which you start, and neither of which carries anything you have said.

Downloading the speech engine

Rustleaf is small; the engine that recognises speech is not. When you choose to set it up, Rustleaf downloads it directly from the projects that publish it — GitHub and Hugging Face. As with any download, those services can see your IP address and which file you asked for. They receive nothing else, and nothing from Rustleaf.

If you installed from the Microsoft Store, the engine comes in the package and this download does not happen at all, unless you later ask for the graphics card version.

Websites you asked Rustleaf to open

If you set up a command that opens a website, saying it opens that site in your browser, exactly as clicking a link would. Rustleaf is not involved beyond opening it.

What Rustleaf never does

Upload your voice or your wordsNever
Send usage statistics or analyticsNever
Ask you to make an accountNever
Show advertisingNever
Sell or share anything with anyoneNever
Record when you are not holding the keysNever

Permissions Rustleaf asks for

  • Your microphone, so it can hear you. Windows asks you the first time, and you can withdraw it whenever you like in Windows privacy settings. Rustleaf only records while you are holding the keys.
  • Watching for the keys you hold. Rustleaf needs to notice your shortcut while you are working in another app, so it watches for that key combination across Windows. It looks only for the keys you chose to hold; it does not read, record or store anything else you type.
  • Typing into other apps. To put your words where your cursor is, Rustleaf pastes into whatever you were typing in, then puts your clipboard back as it was. To decide whether there is somewhere to type at all, it asks Windows what kind of thing currently has your keyboard — not what is written in it.

Children

Rustleaf is not aimed at children and collects nothing about anybody, so there is nothing held about a child or anyone else.

Changes to this policy

If Rustleaf ever changes what it does with your information, this page changes first and the date at the top changes with it. If a future version ever sends anything off your computer, it will ask you plainly and let you say no.
