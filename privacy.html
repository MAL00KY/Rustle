Privacy policy

Last updated 30 September 2026

Rustle does not send your voice, or anything you say, anywhere.

There is no Rustle server and no account. Nothing you dictate is uploaded or analysed, and nobody sees it but you. The speech recognition runs on your own computer.

The rest of this page sets out what the Rustle app touches and where it keeps things, what happens when you buy Rustle Pro, and what this website does, so you can check it for yourself instead of taking it on trust.

What the app records

Your voice

While you hold the keys, Rustle records from your microphone into a temporary file on your computer. It passes that file to the speech engine, which also runs on your machine and cannot be reached from outside it, and then deletes the recording. Nothing is uploaded.

Listening for "Hey Rustle" works differently, and it is switched on when you install Rustle. Rustle can only hear its name if it is already listening, so while this setting is on, the microphone stays open. Up to the last ten minutes of sound are kept in memory and continually written over, so that a long dictation is never cut short. Rustle keeps checking the most recent two or three seconds for its name. Stretches with no voice in them are skipped. The rest are written to a temporary file for the speech engine to read, and deleted as soon as it has read them. None of that sound is saved, it is gone from memory when Rustle closes, and none of it leaves your computer.

If you would rather the microphone stayed closed until you hold the keys, turn off Settings → Commands → Listen for "Hey Rustle" without holding anything.

What you said

Once your speech has been turned into words, Rustle writes those words down so the hub can show your history and word counts. Each entry also records the time, how long you spoke, and the name of the app you were dictating into. Commands you say out loud are recorded the same way.

This is a plain text file on your computer. It is not encrypted, so anyone who can use your Windows account can read it, as they could any document you save. You can delete a single line from the hub, or all of it with Settings → Careful with these → Forget everything I have said.

A log of what Rustle did

So that a problem can be traced, Rustle keeps a log file beside your settings: when it started, which engine it used, how many words it typed and into which app, and anything that went wrong. It does not log your dictation, but it does log the words of commands, and now and then a single name or number from a sentence Rustle Pro chose not to tidy. The log stays on your computer. Nobody sees it unless you send it to someone yourself. If Rustle ever crashes, it writes what went wrong to a file in the Windows temporary folder, which holds no speech.

Your settings and your licence

Your commands, dictionary, snippets and preferences are kept in the same place, so Rustle remembers them. If you buy Rustle Pro, the email address and licence key you enter are stored there as well. Rustle checks the key on your own computer and never sends it anywhere to be checked.

The programs installed on your computer

When you press Add from this PC on the Commands page, Rustle reads the list of programs and games installed on your computer so you can choose which ones to open by voice. It reads the list when you ask, and it does not send it anywhere.

If Wispr Flow does the listening

If you already use Wispr Flow and turn off Use Rustle's own ears, Rustle runs your commands from what you say to Flow instead. To do that it reads Flow's history file on your computer as Flow adds to it, without changing it, and looks for commands in the newest entries. It keeps nothing from that file except the commands it runs, which are recorded in the hub like any other.

Where all of this lives

Everything above is stored on your computer, in one folder:

C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Roaming\Rustle

If you installed Rustle from the Microsoft Store, Windows keeps it in that app's own private folder instead. Settings → Your data → Show me my files opens the right folder either way. You can read, copy, back up or delete any of it whenever you like, and uninstalling Rustle offers to remove it.

What leaves your computer

The app goes online for the reasons below. None of them sends anything you have said or typed.

Downloads you choose

Some parts are too big to include in the app, so Rustle offers to fetch them: a faster speech engine for an NVIDIA graphics card, a larger speech model, and the writing model for Rustle Pro. They come directly from GitHub and Hugging Face, and only when you choose them. As with any download, those services can see your IP address and which file was requested. They receive nothing else from Rustle. Before anything downloaded is used, Rustle checks it is exactly the file its publisher released, and refuses it if not.

Checking for a newer version

A copy of Rustle downloaded from GitHub rather than the Store asks GitHub once a day whether a newer version has been released. The request includes the version number you have, and GitHub can see your IP address, as with any web request. Nothing else is sent. You can turn this off in Settings → Rustle itself → Look for new versions. A copy from the Microsoft Store never does this, because the Store takes care of its updates.

Websites you open

A command that opens a website, or a button in Rustle that links to this site, opens the page in your browser, the same as clicking a link. Rustle does nothing else with it.

What Microsoft tells me

If you installed Rustle from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft gives app makers reports through its developer dashboard: how many people installed the app, from which countries, and technical details of crashes. Windows collects that, not Rustle, under the Microsoft Privacy Statement and your Windows diagnostic settings. The reports do not tell me who you are.

Permissions Rustle asks for

  • Your microphone, so it can hear you. Windows asks the first time, and you can take the permission away at any time in Windows privacy settings. Rustle records while you hold the keys, and keeps the microphone open while "Hey Rustle" is switched on. Your voice, above, explains what happens to that sound.
  • Watching for your keys, so it knows when you are talking to it. This has to work while you are in another app, so Rustle watches the keyboard across Windows. It looks only for the keys you chose, and it does not read or store anything else you type. It also notices which keyboard language is switched on, so it can listen in that language.
  • Typing into other apps, so your words appear where your cursor is. Rustle types them in the way a keyboard would. A few programs, mostly command windows, do not accept that, and for those Rustle pastes the words and then puts your clipboard back the way it was. If there is nowhere to type at all, it leaves the words on your clipboard so you can paste them yourself. To tell whether there is anywhere to type, it asks Windows what kind of box your cursor is in. It never reads what is written there.

Children

Rustle is not made for children. The app collects nothing about anyone, so it holds nothing about children either. If a child has bought Rustle Pro, a parent can write to me for a refund and to have the order details removed.
